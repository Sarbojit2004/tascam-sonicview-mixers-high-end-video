## Tone

**Option 2 (Precise & Technical-but-Accessible) as the spine, with Option 1 (Warm & Trustworthy)
surfacing at the chapter transitions and the CTA.**

The reel scripts blended Option 2 with Option 3's cinematic cadence, because 88 seconds needs a
hook in the first three. Long-form does not have that problem and should not borrow that solution:
a viewer who has chosen a five-minute technical video has already opted in. What they want is
someone who knows the material talking to them like a peer.

So the register here is closer to a systems briefing than a promotional read. Option 2 carries the
engineering. Option 1's consultative warmth is allowed in at the chapter seams — where a listener
needs a moment to resettle — and at the close, where an invitation is genuinely being made.
Option 3's aspirational mode is deliberately not used: at this length it would curdle.

**Delivery notes for the read**

- Slower than the reel read. Around 145–150 wpm. The score is unhurried; match it.
- Every chapter transition gets a real breath. The picture cross-dissolves over ~0.6 s — let it.
- Land figures with a small beat before, not after: *"analog to analog —"* [beat] *"0.51
  milliseconds."*
- "dp" is *dee-pee*, never "dip". "Dante" is *DAHN-tay*. "Cat5e" is *cat five-E*.
- The dp chapter (03:38–04:04) is the emotional centre of this part. Slow further. The line
  "there is no switchover gap to hear, and no reboot to wait through" should be delivered flatly
  and with total confidence — the calm is the point.
- Do not lift into the CTA. It is the same voice, making an offer.
