## Tone

**Option 2 (Precise & Technical-but-Accessible) as the spine, with Option 1 (Warm & Trustworthy)
at the chapter seams, the three case studies, and the CTA.**

Same register as Part 1 — a systems briefing, not a promotional read — but this part has a
different centre of gravity. Part 1 was about a machine; Part 2 is about a working day. Almost
every claim here is a logistics claim rather than an audio one, and the read should not oversell
them into something they are not. "Lighter, and one person can re-pull it" is a genuinely
important sentence in this industry. It does not need to be delivered as if it were a revelation.

The three case-study chapters (03:20 onward, over a third of the runtime) are where Option 1 is
allowed to lead. These are real installations with real constraints, and the warmth belongs there.

**Delivery notes for the read**

- Around 143–148 wpm. Slightly slower than Part 1 through the case studies.
- "Dante" is *DAHN-tay*. "Cat5e" is *cat five-E*. "SB-16D" is *ess-bee sixteen-dee*.
- "IEM" is not used in this part; if the reader improvises, spell it out.
- The line "it is a path that stopped being used" is the thesis of the redundancy chapter. Flat,
  unhurried, no lift on "stopped".
- The SB-16D must never sound like a mixer. Where the script says "the console's input stage,
  moved", the stress is on **moved**.
- Do not lift into the CTA. It is the same voice, making an offer.
