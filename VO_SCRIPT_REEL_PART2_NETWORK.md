## Tone

**Same blend as Part 1: Option 2 (Precise & Technical-but-Accessible) as the base, with Option 3
(Cinematic & Aspirational) cadence on the open and the hand-off.** Consistency across the three
parts matters more than variety — a viewer arriving at Part 2 first should not hear a different
presenter or a different attitude.

One shift within that register: Part 2 contains the series' only genuinely *physical* argument —
the weight and inflexibility of a copper multicore. That section can carry slightly more colour
than anything in Part 1, because it describes something every engineer in the audience has
personally carried up a flight of stairs. It should still be stated, not sold.

**Delivery notes for the read**

- "Dante" is a proper noun, not an acronym — *DAHN-tay*.
- "Cat5e" is read *cat five-E*.
- "SB-16D" is read *S-B sixteen D*.
- The case-study sections (52 s onward) drop a half-step in energy. They are evidence, not
  another pitch — the read should sound like someone citing a reference, not returning to a
  sales point.
- Do not editorialise on the copper-snake line. The list of faults is the argument; adding
  emphasis makes it sound like a complaint.
